# Skymuse: physical model and audit remediation

This revision replaces the three-channel sky approximation with spectral transport and one shared photometric rendering pipeline. It fixes the reproducible implementation defects in `science-authenticity-audit.md`. It does **not** make every preset a measured reconstruction of an actual planet, nor make an SDR monitor reproduce human vision. The limitations below are part of the model specification.

## Units and spectral calculation

Wavelengths run from 360 to 830 nm at 10 nm spacing. Spectral irradiance is W m⁻² nm⁻¹; radiance is W m⁻² sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹. Trapezoidal CIE integration multiplies by 683 lm/W. Its Y component is illuminance for irradiance, luminance for radiance. XYZ is converted to linear sRGB without prematurely clipping negative out-of-gamut components. Display mapping happens after sky, stars and bodies are combined.

The solar boundary spectrum is ASTM G173 extraterrestrial irradiance as tabulated by Bruneton. Other stars use Planck's law, normalized by their bolometric Stefan–Boltzmann flux. `L/Lsun = (R/Rsun)²(T/5772 K)⁴`; AU = 149597870.7 km and nominal solar radius = 695700 km. Solar bolometric irradiance is referenced to 1361 W/m². This is internally consistent to the precision of the nominal solar calibration, not a model of stellar absorption lines, activity, spots, flares, or limb darkening. Cool-star molecular bands are an important unmodelled difference from blackbodies.

Rayleigh columns retain the mixture-weighted hydrostatic approximation `N = p NA/(g M)` with approximate relative molecular cross sections and λ⁻⁴ scaling. These cross-section ratios are not a wavelength-resolved refractive-index database. Ozone uses 1 DU = 2.687×10²⁰ molecules/m². Methane uses the Karkoschka–Tomasko 100/198/296 K table; its logarithmic temperature interpolation is clamped to that measured interval. Outside that interval, coefficients are a proxy, not a validated hot-gas model. The 10 nm mean coefficients cannot resolve saturated narrow lines. Other molecular absorption, collision-induced absorption, and non-LTE processes are omitted.

## Atmospheric transport

The gas and haze each have an exponential density profile, normalized to unit vertical column. Ozone has a prescribed Gaussian layer at 25 km with 8 km width. Clouds have their own Gaussian layer. Radius, scale heights and layer positions enter both viewing and solar paths. Direct paths crossing the reference sphere are blocked. The geometry lookup avoids interpolation across the occultation sentinel. Curved rays use radial Gaussian quadrature with endpoint clustering; geometric comparison rays use quadratic distance sampling. Higher resolution convergence is tested for the clear Earth case.

Single scattering is integrated in spherical geometry with normalized Rayleigh and Henyey–Greenstein phase functions. Incident light is integrated over the stellar solid angle, including partially visible discs. This removes the empirical twilight exponential, artificial forward glow and horizon tint.

Multiple scattering uses a layered hemispheric two-stream solution. Each homogeneous layer has analytic reflection and transmission; the adding recursion sums repeated layer and ground reflections. Source injection includes layer-internal reflection. Conservative layers satisfy R + T = 1; absorbing layers satisfy R + T ≤ 1. The diffuse field feeds a hemispheric source-function reconstruction on spherical viewing rays. The explicit direct first scatter is not counted again as incoming diffuse light. Higher orders vanish relative to first order as the scattering column approaches zero.

**The full angular image is not an exact, energy-conserving 3-D transport solution.** Layer energy conservation does not prove angular reconstruction accuracy. The hemispheric closure is least reliable for strongly anisotropic particles, very thick atmospheres, twilight, horizontally varying illumination and shadow boundaries. Its diffuse field assumes horizontal homogeneity. Refraction changes the direct injection and curved viewing paths, but the hemispheric closure does not solve angular transport through the refractive gradient. The displayed diffuse lux comes from integrating the rendered spectral hemisphere; a separate two-stream flux is retained for diagnostics. Those estimates need not agree exactly. Further validation against DISORT/libRadtran and measured planetary spectra is needed before quoting quantitative scene accuracy.

Thermal emission is Planck radiance times absorption (Kirchhoff's law), using a prescribed isothermal atmosphere and surface. WASP-12b uses an illustrative 2500 K. No self-consistent temperature-pressure profile, atmospheric chemistry, heat transport, convection, or radiative equilibrium is solved.

## Scene geometry and display

The observer is 1.7 m above a spherical reference surface. Local axes are east, zenith and south. Terrain is explicitly illustrative; it is not a geographic reconstruction. The input ground albedo now means the solar photopic mean reflectance of a bounded assumed spectrum, not a Bond albedo. The ground receives the integrated spectral sky plus direct illumination. Reflection into the atmosphere is included by the two-stream boundary condition. Finite-path terrain extinction is integrated; near-terrain inscattering assumes a homogeneous source inferred from the sky. Terrain detail, slope illumination and finite-disc shadows are approximate.

Gas-giant presets are reference cloud levels, not solid surfaces. Procedural terrain is suppressed there. Venus retains a solid surface. Neither the cloud-level boundary nor prescribed weather should be interpreted as a prediction of what an observer could physically survive.

Moon dimensions and distances replace size multipliers. Surfaces use Lambertian reflection with assumed spectral reflectance, not measured BRDFs, terrain maps or resolved geology. Parent-planet and mutual satellite shadows affect companion images and their reflected illumination. Visible disc occultations are rendered, but the atmospheric calculation does **not yet include the reduced primary illumination during solar eclipses** or ring shadows. Rings use a thin plane and prescribed radial optical depths; multiple particle scattering, opposition effects, vertical thickness and planetshine onto the rings are omitted.

All resolved bodies use the same spectral source, foreground extinction, in-scattered foreground and exposure. Catalogue point sources use a normalized pixel PSF. The stellar disc uses angular derivatives and avoids low-angle `acos` precision loss. Perspective and fisheye share angular rays. The fisheye edge is the apparent horizontal plane; its HUD reports zenith rather than a hidden perspective-camera pitch.

“Adapted display” has separate light/dark adaptation time constants, partial LMS chromatic adaptation and approximate night desaturation. It is not a physiological retinal simulation. The SDR tone curve cannot reproduce solar glare, physical contrast, discomfort, adaptation across the visual field, rod spectral response, pupil response, visual acuity or the experience of staring at a star. Fixed-camera gain is independent of sky brightness. No arbitrary lower gain clamp forces WASP daylight to white.

## Astronomy and data provenance

Date/location mode uses Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 for heliocentric planetary positions, Earth/Moon geometry, Galilean satellites and IAU rotation axes. Other named moons use two-body propagation of JPL Horizons osculating states at 2026-01-01 TDB. UTC is converted approximately for those states. The JPL initial states are in `src/science/data/orbits.json`; long-term perturbations and precession are omitted, so these are **approximate moon positions**, especially far from the epoch. Titan/Triton observer frames assume synchronous rotation and an orbit-normal pole. Coordinates use a spherical rather than ellipsoidal observer surface. Aberration, light-time iteration and detailed libration are not fully modelled. Geometric ephemeris directions are refracted by the local atmosphere for display. Exoplanet rotations and observer frames are assumed.

HYG v4.0 supplies a subset of 34,984 stars: valid finite distances and either V magnitude ≤7 or distance ≤100 pc. Coordinates/velocities are propagated from J2000; the visible subset is selected after moving the observer. NASA Exoplanet Archive host positions locate the four exoplanet observers; the Sun is added as a background star from those systems. The catalogue is incomplete from distant hosts. The conversion of V magnitude to photopic lux uses an approximate zero point and blackbody B−V colour. Diffuse Milky Way light, unresolved stars below the catalogue limit, airglow, zodiacal light, aurorae and external planets are not reconstructed.

The catalogue status labels deliberately distinguish measured worlds with assumed weather, hypothetical exoplanet atmospheres, and fictional scenarios. Source values are rounded reference values, not simultaneous measurements or live weather. Numerical error bars are not supplied where the presets have no measured atmospheric constraints. In particular, an Earthlike atmosphere or ocean on TRAPPIST-1e is not asserted.

## Earth comparisons around other stars

Four presets beneath Earth keep its radius, gravity, gas composition, pressure, ozone, haze, clouds, surface reflectance and prescribed temperature. Each has an assumed rotation and observer frame, and can serve as a starting point in the custom editor. Orbital distances scale as `sqrt((L/Lsun)/0.8)` AU, giving each world 1088.8 W/m² of incident bolometric irradiance. The normal Earth preset at 1 AU receives 1361 W/m².

| Host type | Effective temperature (K) | Radius (R☉) | Orbital distance (AU) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| K5V | 4440 | 0.701 | 0.464 |
| M3V | 3430 | 0.361 | 0.143 |
| F5V | 6550 | 1.473 | 2.121 |
| A5V | 8100 | 1.785 | 3.930 |

The temperatures and radii represent average main-sequence stars. The renderer derives their luminosities using the Stefan–Boltzmann law. [Pecaut & Mamajek dwarf sequence](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emamajek/SpectralType/master/EEM_dwarf_UBVIJHK_colors_Teff.txt)

For K, M and F, the habitable-zone boundaries use the one-Earth-mass runaway-greenhouse and maximum-greenhouse fits calibrated for 2600–7200 K. For A, the 8100 K photosphere requires the extended classical fit. That model uses the Leconte inner edge and the CO₂ maximum-greenhouse outer edge and covers 2600–10,000 K. Both models give effective flux limits as a fourth-order polynomial in `T_eff − 5780 K`. The distance for each edge is `sqrt((L/Lsun)/S_eff)` AU. [Kopparapu et al. (2014), Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5292), [Ramirez & Kaltenegger (2018), Table 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.02801), [Ramirez (2018), corrected Table 1](https://doi.org/10.3390/geosciences8080280)

All four orbits lie within their respective fitted ranges. The fixed Earth atmosphere allows a controlled comparison of illumination. The atmosphere and surface temperature of a real planet would also depend on climate, photochemistry and stellar evolution. Changing a preset in the custom editor clears its habitable-zone annotation.

## Atmospheric refraction

`refraction.ts` traces primary rays through a spherical exponential gas. It preserves Bouguer's invariant `n(r) r sin(z)` and integrates radial segments using four-point Gaussian quadrature with sine-squared endpoint spacing. The endpoint transformation resolves grazing tangencies. Inward rays can graze and escape; strong gradients can produce an outward turning level and a ray that returns to the reference surface. Independent Cartesian RK4 tracing verifies the radial geometry.

The same curved paths determine extinction, local scattering directions, stellar illumination and apparent celestial positions. The Sun's shape is obtained by tracing apparent pixel rays back to the geometric stellar cap, so its near-horizon flattening follows the density model. Catalogue stars use the inverse map. Resolved companions and rings use the outgoing asymptotic ray and its spatial offset, retaining their finite distances. Local foreground/terrain rays use a quadratic expansion of the refractive curvature; that approximation is intended for nearby terrain over roughly 35 km and is not a global mirage solution.

Radiance transported between indices follows the `L/n²` invariant; point-source and incident-cap flux also include the solid-angle Jacobian `d(mu_apparent)/d(mu_geometric)`. These are different factors: applying an angular shift alone would give inconsistent total light. The radiance scaling is described in [Physically Based Rendering, 4th edition, section 9.5.2](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Dielectric_BSDF). Thermal source radiance follows the same index convention. The GPU horizon-disc integral agrees with the CPU cap quadrature within 0.04% at the recorded resolution; this verifies internal radiometry, not the atmospheric assumptions.

Position controls and ephemerides remain geometric. The **Atmospheric refraction** switch changes viewing paths, incoming illumination, extinction and star positions together. The Physics panel reports true and apparent centre elevation, or centre occultation when no primary ray exists. Partial-disc light remains integrated when its centre is hidden. With the prescribed Earth profile, a true 0° solar centre appears at approximately +0.492° and the rendered vertical-to-horizontal disc ratio is approximately 0.841. Weather-dependent real refraction will differ.

Refractivity is achromatic at the sodium D reference wavelength, approximately 589 nm. The reference gas coefficients use the ideal-gas column in [Edwards (1919), NBS Technologic Paper 131, Table 1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/nbstechnologic/nbstechnologicpaperT131.pdf). These historical coefficients provide approximate planetary refractivity, not modern precision gas metrology. Water vapour uses Ciddor's dispersion expression, as reproduced in [van der Werf (2003), equation 24](https://siebrenvanderwerf.nl/docs/2003_Refraction_AO.pdf), reduced to the same ideal number density. The Lorentz–Lorenz factor is mixed by mole fraction, scaled with `p/T`, and follows the same exponential scale height as the gas. Nonideal equations of state, wavelength-dependent image dispersion and green flashes, atmospheric inversions, turbulence and multiple images are outside this approximation. Extremely dense presets such as Venus particularly require measured temperature/density profiles and a real-gas equation of state before treating the computed bending as a planetary prediction.

The primary-ray construction follows the spherical refraction invariant and radial integrals discussed by [Mathar (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11808). Validation includes the vacuum limit, unit directions and invariant conservation along paths, Earth high-altitude refraction, and explicit surface-returning rays for the dense Venus profile. Atmospheric profiles remain prescribed rather than measured weather.

## Moonlight and planetshine

Known companions supply a first reflected-light bounce into the observer's atmosphere and ground. Their spectrum uses the same bounded assumed reflectance as their rendered surface. The prescribed companion albedo is interpreted as diffuse surface reflectance; a Lambert sphere with reflectance `rho` has geometric albedo `2 rho / 3`. These simple materials are not measured lunar or planetary phase functions and do not reproduce opposition brightening, clouds, continents or non-Lambertian lunar regolith.

The incident spectral irradiance is `E_reflected(lambda) = E_star(lambda) rho(lambda) (2/3) (R/d)^2 Phi(alpha)`, where `Phi(alpha) = [sin(alpha) + (pi-alpha) cos(alpha)]/pi` and alpha is the star–body–observer phase angle. The standard Lambert-sphere normalization and phase law are given by [Seager, Whitney & Sasselov (2000), section 2](https://lweb.cfa.harvard.edu/~sasselov/exopl/p2.pdf). Independent projected-disc surface integration tests the normalization at several phases. Finite-distance corrections to that far-field irradiance law are not applied; the resolved sphere still uses finite-distance geometry.

A finite stellar-cap and visible-body quadrature estimates the fraction of reflected light lost to parent-planet or mutual companion shadows. A new or fully shadowed body supplies no artificial minimum illumination. For atmospheric transfer and local ground shadows each companion is an equivalent uniform disc carrying its integrated phase-dependent flux; the resolved crescent brightness pattern is not used as a spatial illumination map. The same refraction, extinction and scattering model used for starlight acts on these sources.

With fixed optical properties, the transfer problem is linear in incident light. Each source is solved in its own axisymmetric frame and its field is rotated and added into a full-azimuth sky texture. The individual hemispheric irradiances are summed for telemetry and ground reflection. This preserves the companion's sky direction rather than imposing mirror symmetry around the primary star. Thermal emission is included only once. Ground shadows use each source direction. Worker results retain their observation/companion snapshot while a new calculation is pending.

This is a single stellar-reflection bounce. Repeated Earth–Moon illumination, earthshine on the Moon's dark face, reflection between companions, ring illumination of the atmosphere, and wavelength-dependent companion atmospheres are not solved. A companion's incident stellar spectrum is approximated by that at the observer's orbital radius, and its atmospheric illumination directions are taken from the observer rather than recomputing parallax at every scattering point. These approximations matter more for close or very large bodies and near rising/setting crescents. The diffuse atmospheric closure retains the angular and horizontal-homogeneity limits described above.

## Custom world inputs

The **Customise** editor builds a separate custom scenario from an existing preset. A stellar choice carries its radius, temperature, spectrum type and, where known, catalogue host position. Custom stars explicitly use blackbody spectra, even if a user names one “Sol”. Luminosity continues to be derived from radius and temperature. Orbital distance is in AU and must keep the planet outside the stellar photosphere.

Gas percentages are mole/volume fractions. Mean molar mass is their weighted molecular mass. Surface pressure and density are linked using `rho = p M / (R T)` with SI units; isothermal scale height is `H = R T / (M g)`. The editor keeps either pressure or density independent, according to the selected mode. Switching modes preserves the existing atmosphere. Molecular masses use conventional values from the [NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69](https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/). The same derived quantities drive molecular columns and spherical path geometry; they are not additional cosmetic controls.

The editor requires nonnegative percentages totalling 100% for a nonzero atmosphere. It validates finite values, stellar overlap, pressure up to 300 bar, and the supported scale-height range (0.1–1000 km, at most one quarter of the planet radius). Zero pressure/density disables gas scattering, haze, cloud extinction and ozone. Temperature remains a prescribed atmosphere/surface temperature rather than a solved equilibrium climate. Real-gas density corrections, condensation and chemistry remain outside this model, particularly relevant to dense or very cold mixtures.

Haze and cloud phase functions and single-scattering albedos are inherited from the starting preset; editable optical depths and vertical profiles set their amount and location. Surface albedo sets total reflected light; colour is an approximate tint and desaturates where necessary to remain a bounded reflectance. A tiny neutral spectral component allows black or saturated input colours to retain the requested nonzero albedo.

Custom worlds have their own identity and user-authored provenance. They do not inherit catalogue moons or Saturn's fixed annuli. They use manual stellar geometry so planetary ephemerides cannot overwrite the chosen distance. The background star catalogue relocates to a known selected host when possible; arbitrary custom stars use an assumed reference observer frame. One validated custom scenario is saved locally in the browser. Invalid or obsolete saved data fall back to the default preset.

## Verification

Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, and open `/tests/sky-render.html` on the Vite server. The browser harness uses the production shader and floating-point framebuffers. Results and scene galleries are in `docs/science-fix-evidence/`, `docs/refraction-evidence/` and `docs/companion-evidence/`. Node tests cover spectral references, dimensional laws, columns, phase-function normalization, energy conservation, thin limits, angular convergence, catalogue consistency, ephemerides, independent refraction geometry, reflected-light phase normalization, shadows and linear superposition. Browser checks measure stellar radiance/flux, refracted image flattening and flux, catalogue refraction, actual fisheye projection, opaque-atmosphere compositing, companion phases, surface normals, integrated reflected flux, secondary sky/ground illumination and shader errors. The transparent refractive reference shown in the harness deliberately removes Earth's extinction to isolate geometry and radiometry; it is not a prediction of a clear Earth sunset.

The earlier audit report and `science-audit-evidence/` deliberately preserve the **pre-fix** implementation's measurements. Their scripts refer to the baseline API and should be run against baseline commit `648e2d6`, not treated as current regression tests. Passing the new checks verifies specified invariants; it is not empirical validation of every preset's colour, climate or perceptual appearance.
