# Skymuse: physical model and audit remediation

This revision replaces the three-channel sky approximation with spectral transport and one shared photometric rendering pipeline. It fixes the reproducible implementation defects in `science-authenticity-audit.md`. It does **not** make every preset a measured reconstruction of an actual planet, nor make an SDR monitor reproduce human vision. The limitations below are part of the model specification.

## Units and spectral calculation

Wavelengths run from 360 to 830 nm at 10 nm spacing. Spectral irradiance is W m⁻² nm⁻¹; radiance is W m⁻² sr⁻¹ nm⁻¹. Trapezoidal CIE integration multiplies by 683 lm/W. Its Y component is illuminance for irradiance, luminance for radiance. XYZ is converted to linear sRGB without prematurely clipping negative out-of-gamut components. Display mapping happens after sky, stars and bodies are combined.

The solar boundary spectrum is ASTM G173 extraterrestrial irradiance as tabulated by Bruneton. Other stars use Planck's law, normalized by their bolometric Stefan–Boltzmann flux. `L/Lsun = (R/Rsun)²(T/5772 K)⁴`; AU = 149597870.7 km and nominal solar radius = 695700 km. Solar bolometric irradiance is referenced to 1361 W/m². This is internally consistent to the precision of the nominal solar calibration, not a model of stellar absorption lines, activity, spots, flares, or limb darkening. Cool-star molecular bands are an important unmodelled difference from blackbodies.

Rayleigh columns retain the mixture-weighted hydrostatic approximation `N = p NA/(g M)` with approximate relative molecular cross sections and λ⁻⁴ scaling. These cross-section ratios are not a wavelength-resolved refractive-index database. Ozone uses 1 DU = 2.687×10²⁰ molecules/m². Methane uses the Karkoschka–Tomasko 100/198/296 K table; its logarithmic temperature interpolation is clamped to that measured interval. Outside that interval, coefficients are a proxy, not a validated hot-gas model. The 10 nm mean coefficients cannot resolve saturated narrow lines. Other molecular absorption, collision-induced absorption, and non-LTE processes are omitted.

## Atmospheric transport

The gas and haze each have an exponential density profile, normalized to unit vertical column. Ozone has a prescribed Gaussian layer at 25 km with 8 km width. Clouds have their own Gaussian layer. Radius, scale heights and layer positions enter both viewing and solar paths. Direct paths crossing the reference sphere are blocked. The geometry lookup avoids interpolation across the occultation sentinel. Quadratic distance sampling resolves the lower atmosphere; higher resolution convergence is tested for the clear Earth case.

Single scattering is integrated in spherical geometry with normalized Rayleigh and Henyey–Greenstein phase functions. Incident light is integrated over the stellar solid angle, including partially visible discs. This removes the empirical twilight exponential, artificial forward glow and horizon tint.

Multiple scattering uses a layered hemispheric two-stream solution. Each homogeneous layer has analytic reflection and transmission; the adding recursion sums repeated layer and ground reflections. Source injection includes layer-internal reflection. Conservative layers satisfy R + T = 1; absorbing layers satisfy R + T ≤ 1. The diffuse field feeds a hemispheric source-function reconstruction on spherical viewing rays. The explicit direct first scatter is not counted again as incoming diffuse light. Higher orders vanish relative to first order as the scattering column approaches zero.

**The full angular image is not an exact, energy-conserving 3-D transport solution.** Layer energy conservation does not prove angular reconstruction accuracy. The hemispheric closure is least reliable for strongly anisotropic particles, very thick atmospheres, twilight, horizontally varying illumination and shadow boundaries. Its diffuse field assumes horizontal homogeneity. The displayed diffuse lux comes from integrating the rendered spectral hemisphere; a separate two-stream flux is retained for diagnostics. Those estimates need not agree exactly. Further validation against DISORT/libRadtran and measured planetary spectra is needed before quoting quantitative scene accuracy.

Thermal emission is Planck radiance times absorption (Kirchhoff's law), using a prescribed isothermal atmosphere and surface. WASP-12b uses an illustrative 2500 K. No self-consistent temperature-pressure profile, atmospheric chemistry, heat transport, convection, or radiative equilibrium is solved.

## Scene geometry and display

The observer is 1.7 m above a spherical reference surface. Local axes are east, zenith and south. Terrain is explicitly illustrative; it is not a geographic reconstruction. The input ground albedo now means the solar photopic mean reflectance of a bounded assumed spectrum, not a Bond albedo. The ground receives the integrated spectral sky plus direct illumination. Reflection into the atmosphere is included by the two-stream boundary condition. Finite-path terrain extinction is integrated; near-terrain inscattering assumes a homogeneous source inferred from the sky. Terrain detail, slope illumination and finite-disc shadows are approximate.

Gas-giant presets are reference cloud levels, not solid surfaces. Procedural terrain is suppressed there. Venus retains a solid surface. Neither the cloud-level boundary nor prescribed weather should be interpreted as a prediction of what an observer could physically survive.

Moon dimensions and distances replace size multipliers. Surfaces use Lambertian reflection with assumed reflectance colour, not measured BRDFs, terrain maps or resolved geology. Planet shadows and visible disc occultations are rendered; the atmospheric calculation does **not yet include the reduced illumination during solar eclipses**, mutual satellite shadows, moonlight/planetshine as secondary sky/ground illuminants, or ring shadows on the atmosphere. Rings use a thin plane and prescribed radial optical depths; multiple particle scattering, opposition effects, vertical thickness and planetshine are omitted.

All resolved bodies use the same spectral source, foreground extinction, in-scattered foreground and exposure. Catalogue point sources use a normalized pixel PSF. The stellar disc uses angular derivatives and avoids low-angle `acos` precision loss. Perspective and fisheye share angular rays. The fisheye edge is the geometric horizon; its HUD reports zenith rather than a hidden perspective-camera pitch.

“Adapted display” has separate light/dark adaptation time constants, partial LMS chromatic adaptation and approximate night desaturation. It is not a physiological retinal simulation. The SDR tone curve cannot reproduce solar glare, physical contrast, discomfort, adaptation across the visual field, rod spectral response, pupil response, visual acuity or the experience of staring at a star. Fixed-camera gain is independent of sky brightness. No arbitrary lower gain clamp forces WASP daylight to white.

## Astronomy and data provenance

Date/location mode uses Astronomy Engine 2.1.19 for heliocentric planetary positions, Earth/Moon geometry, Galilean satellites and IAU rotation axes. Other named moons use two-body propagation of JPL Horizons osculating states at 2026-01-01 TDB. UTC is converted approximately for those states. The JPL initial states are in `src/science/data/orbits.json`; long-term perturbations and precession are omitted, so these are **approximate moon positions**, especially far from the epoch. Titan/Triton observer frames assume synchronous rotation and an orbit-normal pole. Coordinates use a spherical rather than ellipsoidal observer surface. Aberration, light-time iteration, atmospheric refraction and detailed libration are not fully modelled. Exoplanet rotations and observer frames are assumed.

HYG v4.0 supplies a subset of 34,984 stars: valid finite distances and either V magnitude ≤7 or distance ≤100 pc. Coordinates/velocities are propagated from J2000; the visible subset is selected after moving the observer. NASA Exoplanet Archive host positions locate the four exoplanet observers; the Sun is added as a background star from those systems. The catalogue is incomplete from distant hosts. The conversion of V magnitude to photopic lux uses an approximate zero point and blackbody B−V colour. Diffuse Milky Way light, unresolved stars below the catalogue limit, airglow, zodiacal light, aurorae and external planets are not reconstructed.

The catalogue status labels deliberately distinguish measured worlds with assumed weather, hypothetical exoplanet atmospheres, and fictional scenarios. Source values are rounded reference values, not simultaneous measurements or live weather. Numerical error bars are not supplied where the presets have no measured atmospheric constraints. In particular, an Earthlike atmosphere or ocean on TRAPPIST-1e is not asserted.

## Earth comparisons around K and M dwarfs

Two presets appear directly beneath Earth in the world picker. They copy Earth's radius, gravity, pressure, gas composition, ozone, haze, cloud settings, surface reflectance and prescribed temperature. They have separate identities and assumed observer frames; they do not inherit Earth's lunar ephemeris or orbital distance. They also remain available as starting points in the custom editor.

The representative stellar parameters come from [Pecaut & Mamajek (2013), online dwarf sequence version 2022.04.16](https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~emamajek/EEM_dwarf_UBVIJHK_colors_Teff.txt): K5V at 4440 K and 0.701 R☉, M3V at 3430 K and 0.361 R☉. The renderer continues to derive luminosity from radius and temperature and uses blackbody spectra. These are representative classes, not observations of specific planetary systems.

Orbital distance is `sqrt((L/Lsun)/0.8)` AU. Each preset therefore receives 1088.8 W/m², or 80% of the 1361 W/m² Earth reference. Distances are approximately 0.464 AU (K dwarf) and 0.143 AU (M dwarf).

The distances are checked against the conservative one-Earth-mass runaway-greenhouse inner edge and maximum-greenhouse outer edge from [Kopparapu et al. (2014), equations 4–5 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1404.5292). `S_eff = S_sun + a T* + b T*² + c T*³ + d T*⁴`, with `T* = T_eff − 5780 K`, and `d_HZ = sqrt((L/Lsun)/S_eff)` AU. The climate-fit reference temperature is distinct from the renderer's 5772 K nominal solar luminosity calibration. The fit is only evaluated within its published 2600–7200 K range. The Physics panel shows the resulting distance interval.

Equal Earth bolometric irradiance would exceed the conservative inner-edge flux for these cool stars; 0.8 Earth flux supplies a margin within both intervals. Habitable-zone placement is a model criterion for possible liquid surface water under favourable atmospheric conditions. It does not solve the retained Earth's climate, photochemistry, atmospheric retention, stellar activity, rotation or tidal locking. Changing a comparison in the custom editor clears its preset habitable-zone annotation rather than retaining a potentially invalid claim.

## Custom world inputs

The **Customise** editor builds a separate custom scenario from an existing preset. A stellar choice carries its radius, temperature, spectrum type and, where known, catalogue host position. Custom stars explicitly use blackbody spectra, even if a user names one “Sol”. Luminosity continues to be derived from radius and temperature. Orbital distance is in AU and must keep the planet outside the stellar photosphere.

Gas percentages are mole/volume fractions. Mean molar mass is their weighted molecular mass. Surface pressure and density are linked using `rho = p M / (R T)` with SI units; isothermal scale height is `H = R T / (M g)`. The editor keeps either pressure or density independent, according to the selected mode. Switching modes preserves the existing atmosphere. Molecular masses use conventional values from the [NIST Chemistry WebBook, SRD 69](https://webbook.nist.gov/chemistry/). The same derived quantities drive molecular columns and spherical path geometry; they are not additional cosmetic controls.

The editor requires nonnegative percentages totalling 100% for a nonzero atmosphere. It validates finite values, stellar overlap, pressure up to 300 bar, and the supported scale-height range (0.1–1000 km, at most one quarter of the planet radius). Zero pressure/density disables gas scattering, haze, cloud extinction and ozone. Temperature remains a prescribed atmosphere/surface temperature rather than a solved equilibrium climate. Real-gas density corrections, condensation and chemistry remain outside this model, particularly relevant to dense or very cold mixtures.

Haze and cloud phase functions and single-scattering albedos are inherited from the starting preset; editable optical depths and vertical profiles set their amount and location. Surface albedo sets total reflected light; colour is an approximate tint and desaturates where necessary to remain a bounded reflectance. A tiny neutral spectral component allows black or saturated input colours to retain the requested nonzero albedo.

Custom worlds have their own identity and user-authored provenance. They do not inherit catalogue moons or Saturn's fixed annuli. They use manual stellar geometry so planetary ephemerides cannot overwrite the chosen distance. The background star catalogue relocates to a known selected host when possible; arbitrary custom stars use an assumed reference observer frame. One validated custom scenario is saved locally in the browser. Invalid or obsolete saved data fall back to the default preset.

## Verification

Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, and open `/tests/sky-render.html` on the Vite server. The browser harness uses the production shader and floating-point framebuffers. Results and a scene gallery are in `docs/science-fix-evidence/`. Node tests cover spectral references, dimensional laws, columns, phase-function normalization, energy conservation, thin limits, angular convergence, catalogue consistency and ephemerides. Browser checks measure stellar radiance/flux, actual fisheye projection, opaque-atmosphere compositing, companion phases and shader errors.

The earlier audit report and `science-audit-evidence/` deliberately preserve the **pre-fix** implementation's measurements. Their scripts refer to the baseline API and should be run against baseline commit `648e2d6`, not treated as current regression tests. Passing the new checks verifies specified invariants; it is not empirical validation of every preset's colour, climate or perceptual appearance.
